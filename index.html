Play money, fair by construction. Doubloons aren't real money. Every boat is priced the same way — longshots win less often and pay more, and over time every bet returns 97% of what's staked. In a Tide race the winner comes from a block that doesn't exist yet when you bet, so nobody can know it or rig it, and anyone can check it afterwards.

For the curious — how the tide decides

The tide is Blake's testnet4 — the BLAKE2b hardfork of Bitcoin, which parted from plain testnet4 at block 150,308. From there a header is 164 bytes and a block hash is BLAKE2b rather than double-SHA256, and mempool.guide is the explorer that follows it. Which chain matters to the proof: above the fork the same height names a different block on plain testnet4, so check a race on Blake or you will be comparing against another chain's history.

In Tide mode your bet names a Blake block that does not exist yet. When that block is mined, the race seed is sha256(blockHash | mark) — your mark is a random nonce fixed at bet time. The seed picks a number on a 10,000-point line; each boat owns a band of it sized by its odds. Nobody — not you, not this page, not anyone — knows the winner before the tide comes in, and anyone can recompute it afterwards from public chain data. The verify ✓ in the log does exactly that, in your browser. Races settle only after the deciding block has a confirmation on top of it — testnet4 reorgs are real (we watched one orphan a race's block), and if a deeper reorg ever moves a settled block, verify says so instead of pretending. Races logged before the fleet moved to Blake are marked as the plain-testnet4 races they were, and still verify against the chain they settled on.

Even the race replay is honest: every gust and stall is drawn from the same seed, so two replays of the same race are identical, anywhere.

Practice mode is seeded locally and marked as such — instant, but not provable.
